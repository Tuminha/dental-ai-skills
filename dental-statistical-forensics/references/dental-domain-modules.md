# Dental Domain Modules

Select the modules that match the paper. Do not run irrelevant modules.

## Esthetic Zone / Ridge Preservation

Check:

- Horizontal ridge-width loss.
- Mid-buccal and interproximal vertical changes.
- Buccal contour and facial-wall failure.
- Soft-tissue thickness and midfacial recession.
- PES/WES or other esthetic acceptability outcomes.
- Need for additional GBR, connective-tissue grafting, staged augmentation, or altered implant plan.
- Proportion crossing failure thresholds.
- Mean benefit with high SD/IQR/range.
- "Predictability" claims unsupported by individual data.

Key red flag:

> Favorable mean dimensional change does not prove predictable esthetic-zone anatomy if SD/range includes clinically relevant collapse.

## Sinus Lift / Grafting

Check:

- Residual bone height baseline balance.
- Vertical bone gain and graft shrinkage/remodeling.
- Membrane perforation and complication rates per patient/sinus.
- Implant survival vs success.
- Follow-up after loading, not only after implant placement.
- Bilateral sinus or multiple implant clustering.
- Histology/core missingness and graft-material remnants.
- Graft percentage interpreted separately as vital bone, non-vital graft, connective tissue, and total mineralized tissue.

## Periodontal Treatment

Check:

- Probing-depth reduction.
- CAL gain.
- Pocket closure percentage.
- Bleeding on probing.
- Patient/tooth/site-level clustering.
- Full-mouth vs selected-site reporting.
- Maintenance interval and adherence.
- Smoking/diabetes stratification.
- Mean PD/CAL change vs proportion reaching clinically useful pocket closure.
- Examiner calibration and probing measurement error.

Key red flag:

> Site-level data nested within teeth and patients cannot be treated as independent observations.

## Implant Outcomes

Check:

- Survival vs success.
- Patient-level vs implant-level reporting.
- Marginal bone loss mean/SD/range.
- Biological and technical complications.
- Time under loading.
- Dropouts and censored implants.
- Multiple implants per patient/operator/center.
- Industry-sponsored device outcome claims.

Key red flag:

> High implant survival does not equal high success if complications, marginal bone loss, or peri-implant disease are not accounted for.

## Digital Dentistry

Check:

- Trueness vs precision.
- RMS and angular deviation.
- Full-arch vs short-span indirectness.
- Scan body position and scan path.
- Repeated scans clustered by model/patient/operator.
- In-vitro vs in-vivo indirectness.
- Color-map overinterpretation.
- Clinically acceptable threshold and whether it is validated for the specific span/prosthesis.

## Diagnostic Accuracy

Check:

- Sensitivity/specificity with CI.
- Threshold predefinition.
- ROC/AUC interpretation.
- Reference-standard quality.
- Prevalence effects on PPV/NPV.
- Indeterminate results.
- Estimate-level QUADAS-3 thinking.
- Patient/site/tooth/surface diagnostic-unit mismatch.

Key red flag:

> Diagnostic accuracy estimates without CIs or clear diagnostic units are not enough for clinical confidence.

## Meta-Analysis

Check:

- Pooling compatible designs, outcomes, units, and time points.
- Random-effects vs fixed-effect rationale.
- Heterogeneity and prediction intervals.
- Small-study/publication bias.
- Subgroup/meta-regression overreach.
- Unit-of-analysis overlap across included studies.
- SMD interpretability and back-translation to clinical units.
- Whether pooled result hides clinically important dispersion between studies.

Key red flag:

> A statistically significant pooled average can still be clinically unreliable if heterogeneity is high or prediction intervals cross clinically important thresholds.
