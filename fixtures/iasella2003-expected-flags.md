# Expected Flags: Iasella 2003 Ridge Preservation Fixture

These are known-answer checks for the compact Iasella-style regression fixture. The goal is not exact wording; the goal is to preserve the critical numerical interpretation.

## Must Flag

- Favorable **average** dimensional effect for ridge preservation.
- Horizontal ridge-width loss appears smaller in ridge preservation than extraction alone.
- Mid-buccal vertical mean favors ridge preservation, but dispersion is large.
- Ridge-preservation mid-buccal SD (+/- 2.0 mm) is larger than the mean gain (+1.3 mm).
- Ridge-preservation mid-buccal range includes clinically relevant loss (-2.0 mm), so some preserved sites still lost buccal height.
- Extraction-alone horizontal SD (+/- 2.3 mm) is very large, indicating high variability in the control group.
- Final ridge-width means are not enough to infer predictable implant-site adequacy for every patient/site.
- The correct interpretation is "average risk reduction / average benefit," not guaranteed esthetic-zone preservation.
- A "predictable maintenance" claim must be softened unless supported by responder analysis, patient/site-level success thresholds, or narrow prediction intervals.
- Clinical planning should still account for case-specific augmentation, soft-tissue management, and esthetic risk.

## Should Request or Compute If Data Permit

- 95% CI for between-group differences if group n and SDs are confirmed.
- Proportion of sites crossing clinically important failure thresholds, if individual data are available.
- Prediction interval or responder analysis when individual predictability is the clinical claim.
- Clarification of whether measurements are patient-level, tooth/site-level, or socket-level.
- Measurement reliability / examiner calibration / radiographic or cast-measurement error.

## Must Not Do

- Do not conclude that ridge preservation is "predictable" from the mean alone.
- Do not treat statistical significance as clinical reliability.
- Do not make a practice-change recommendation from one short-term study alone.
- Do not invent CIs, PMIDs, DOIs, or article metadata that are not supplied or verified.
