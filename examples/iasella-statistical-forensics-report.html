Major Flags

Dispersion undermines individual predictability
Favorable means do not guarantee esthetic-zone preservation for an individual patient. The mid-buccal RP SD was larger than the mean gain, and the reported range included clinically relevant loss.
Horizontal result is statistically fragile
The horizontal effect favored ridge preservation, but the reported P value was near the significance boundary and an approximate CI crosses zero.
Histology missingness likely informative
Two extraction-site trephine cores could not be obtained because of minimal bone fill, and both were smokers. Excluding these sites may make the histology comparison look better than the full clinical reality.
More bone is biologically ambiguous
Total bone in the ridge-preservation group includes non-vital graft particles. Vital bone and total mineralized tissue should not be interpreted as the same biological outcome.

Clinical Interpretation

Ridge preservation appears to reduce average collapse, but it should be interpreted as risk reduction rather than predictable preservation. In esthetic-zone planning, the paper does not remove the need for patient-specific augmentation planning.

Claim Discipline

A defensible conclusion is: ridge preservation shifted average dimensional outcomes in a favorable direction. A less defensible conclusion is: ridge preservation predictably maintains esthetic-zone anatomy for individual patients.

Limitations

Small sample, short healing endpoint, no long-term implant outcomes, missing extraction histology data, and limited ability to estimate patient-level failure probabilities.
